### Reflection
---
During the PC Assemble lab, I had the opportunity to deepen my understanding of computer architecture through a hands-on session focused on the fundamentals of PC hardware. Working with an older workstation, I practiced the systematic process of dismantling and reassembling various components, which provided a clear, practical look at how parts like the motherboard, CPU, and RAM interface within a physical system. This exercise was essential for bridging the gap between theoretical diagrams and real-world hardware, emphasizing the importance of careful handling and proper assembly sequences. As a Graphics and Multimedia student, gaining this foundational knowledge of the insides of a computer is invaluable, as it helps me better appreciate the hardware capabilities required to power demanding design and rendering software. Upgrading to newer systems would allow students to engage with current industry standards, such as M.2 NVMe storage installation, DDR5 memory configurations, and modern cable management solutions found in contemporary cases. Transitioning from older hardware to up-to-date parts would better prepare us for the high-performance specifications required in the Graphics and Multimedia industry.
